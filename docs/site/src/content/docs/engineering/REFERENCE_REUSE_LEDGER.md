This ledger records Director's source-reuse decisions for the eight repositories in
the competitive-union study. It is an engineering control, not legal advice and not
a replacement for the upstream license text.

## Rules

- Copying a small function, translating it, renaming symbols, or substantially
  rewriting its structure is still source reuse. The applicable upstream license
  and this ledger still apply.
- Director may make focused use of MIT or Apache-2.0 source when the reuse is useful,
  reviewed, and attributed. Do not import an upstream subtree or product wholesale.
- Before merging reused source, add an entry to the **Active source-copy register**
  with the exact upstream revision and path, local destination, license, notices,
  and a summary of modifications.
- Code and assets are separate decisions. Do not infer permission for models,
  textures, motion clips, fonts, audio, video, logos, presets, or bundled binaries
  from a repository's code-license label. Record the exact asset and its own terms.
- For a clean-room-only reference, implement the documented behavior in Director's
  own model and architecture. Do not translate, lightly rewrite, or structurally
  reproduce upstream implementation. “Clean-room” here describes project policy;
  it is not a legal conclusion about any particular implementation.
- If a license or asset origin is unclear, the default is **do not copy or
  redistribute** until the record is resolved.

## Maintenance gate

A reference-derived change is not provenance-complete until review can answer all of
these questions:

1. Is this behavior-only learning, copied/adapted source, or an imported asset?
2. What exact upstream revision and paths were examined or reused?
3. Does the local file retain every required copyright, license, NOTICE, and
   modification statement?
4. If it is an asset, who created it and may Director redistribute and modify it?
5. Does the project distribution model satisfy copyleft, non-commercial, patent,
   trademark, codec, and binary obligations?

When any answer is unknown, keep the implementation independent and the asset out of
redistributable packages until the uncertainty is resolved.
